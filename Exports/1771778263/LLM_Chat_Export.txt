## 7.  Key take‑aways

1. **Displacement is real but not universal.** Routine, rule‑based jobs are most vulnerable; high‑skill, creative, and interpersonal roles are largely protected.
2. **Augmentation dominates.** AI will most often *assist* rather than *replace*, creating hybrid roles that combine human judgment with machine speed.
3. **New jobs will outnumber lost ones**—but only if the economy and policy frameworks adapt quickly enough to absorb displaced workers.
4. **Societal impact hinges on policy.** Without proactive measures (education, safety nets, ethics), AI could widen inequality and erode social cohesion.
5. **The next 20 years will be a transition period.** By 2046, many industries will have re‑imagined their workforce, but the pace and equity of that transition will vary widely across regions and sectors.

### Final thought

AI will **replace** certain jobs, **augment** many others, and **create** new ones. The net effect on employment will be shaped by how quickly new roles emerge, how effectively workers are retrained, and how governments and societies choose to distribute the gains. The next two decades will test our ability to turn a powerful technological shift into a broadly inclusive economic transformation.

## 1.  The macro‑picture

| What AI can do | What it can’t do (as of 2026) | Likely net effect on employment |
|----------------|------------------------------|---------------------------------|
| **Automate routine, rule‑based tasks** (e.g., data entry, basic diagnostics) | **High‑level judgment, empathy, creativity, and complex physical interaction** | **Shift, not collapse** – many jobs will be *augmented* rather than *eliminated*. |
| **Scale productivity** (24/7 operations, faster data analysis) | **Human oversight for safety, ethics, and trust** | **Higher wages for high‑skill roles, lower wages for routine roles**. |
| **Create new roles** (AI trainers, ethicists, data curators, AI‑augmented specialists) | **Demand for soft skills (critical thinking, collaboration, emotional intelligence)** | **Job growth in “AI‑support” and “human‑centered” fields**. |

**Bottom line:** AI will *replace* some jobs, *augment* many others, and *create* new ones. The net employment effect depends on how quickly new roles materialize, how policy shapes the transition, and how the economy absorbs productivity gains.
